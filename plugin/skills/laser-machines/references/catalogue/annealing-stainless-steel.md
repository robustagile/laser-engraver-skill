## Acceptance criteria

- Deep black, not grey and not brown, judged straight on and at 45 degrees — an annealed mark
  that looks black only from one angle has an oxide layer of the wrong thickness.
- **Zero material removal.** A depth gauge reads nothing and the surface is still smooth to a
  fingernail. Removal means it stopped being annealing.
- Even across the whole marked area, including where the fill changes direction.
- Survives a wipe with isopropanol: what wipes off was soot, not an annealed mark.
- The passive layer is intact: no rust develops where a drop of tap water is left to dry on the
  mark over 24 hours. This is the reason annealing is chosen over etching on stainless, so it is
  a criterion and not a nicety.

## How to verify

Depth gauge, then eye in diffuse daylight from both angles, then the isopropanol wipe, then the
water drop left overnight on a coupon. All four; the first three take a minute and the last runs
while something else is happening.
