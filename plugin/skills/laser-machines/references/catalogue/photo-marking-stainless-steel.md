## Acceptance criteria

- **A ten-step greyscale wedge burned beside the image shows ten distinguishable steps.** This is
  the criterion the whole entry rests on: a photo that looks acceptable while the wedge collapses
  into three or four tones is a two-tone mark that happens to suit that photograph, and it will
  fail on the next one.
- Neither end of the range is clipped: the lightest step is distinguishable from bare material,
  and the darkest from the step above it.
- No banding along the scan direction, and no moiré in areas of even tone, at the distance the
  piece is actually looked at.
- Tone is consistent across the field: the same wedge burned at field centre and near a corner
  reads the same.
- Repeatable — the second run of the same file is not visibly lighter or darker than the first.

- Tone comes from oxide colour rather than from removal: a depth gauge finds nothing across the
  darkest area.

Greyscale on stainless is an annealing result driven pixel by pixel, which is why this is a MOPA
entry: the tonal range comes from pulse duration and frequency, and a machine without those
levers has power and speed alone to produce it with.

## How to verify

Burn the ten-step wedge in the same file as the image, every time, and judge the wedge before the
image — the eye forgives a photograph and does not forgive a wedge. Compare wedges from field
centre and a corner. Depth gauge across the darkest area to confirm nothing was removed.

Both dithered and greyscale modes need their own recipe: the same material and the same machine
give different settings for the two, and a recipe verified for one says nothing about the other.
