## Acceptance criteria

The other answer to contrast on aluminium: a **laser-bonding ink** (Brilliance Laser Inks and
similar) is applied over the mark, a pass bonds it where the beam goes, and the unbonded rest is
washed off. It differs from `whitening-engraved-floor-aluminium` in kind — it adds a material
rather than texturing one — and it is the route to a genuinely black mark on aluminium, which
the laser alone cannot produce.

It applies both to a deep-engraved cut that needs filling and to a flat surface with no cut at
all. The ink manufacturer's own instructions govern surface preparation, application and washing;
what is calibrated here is only the **bonding pass**.

- The bonded mark is black and even, with no thin or missed patches, including inside the corners
  and along the walls of a cut.
- No ink remains bonded outside the intended geometry, and the wash removes everything unbonded
  without scrubbing the part.
- The bond survives: an isopropanol wipe, a fingernail scrape and, where the job needs it,
  whatever cleaning the part will really see.
- **On a regulatory mark, the cut alone must remain compliant and legible without the ink.** The
  ink is contrast, never the thing that makes the marking meet its requirement — it can wear off,
  and the depth criterion is measured before it goes on.
- Underlying depth is unchanged by the bonding pass.

**Extraction is mandatory for this work**, and the agent states that before the first burn: this
is lasering a chemical coating, not bare metal, and the fumes are the coating's, not the
material's (R-S1).

## How to verify

Judge coverage under a strong light, looking into the cut and along its walls, not only straight
down. Wash and dry, then the isopropanol wipe and the fingernail. Depth gauge before application
and after bonding.

Prepare the coupon exactly as the part will be prepared — degreasing matters more here than any
laser parameter, and a bonding recipe verified on a coupon that was cleaned differently is not
transferable.
