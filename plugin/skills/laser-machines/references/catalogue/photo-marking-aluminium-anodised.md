## Acceptance criteria

Anodised aluminium is the **easiest and most forgiving photo substrate of the three**, and it
works the opposite way round from bare metal. The laser bleaches dye out of the anodic coating,
so more energy means a *lighter* tone on a dark ground: the image is effectively inverted
relative to a photo on stainless, where more energy means darker. The tonal range lives entirely
inside a coating some 0.01 to 0.05 mm thick, which is why the window is narrow and why, once
found, it repeats so well. It is also why this entry is not MOPA-only — the range comes from dye
removal rather than from oxide colour, so a plain fiber machine reaches it too.

- **A ten-step greyscale wedge burned beside the image shows ten distinguishable steps**, from
  untouched coating at one end to fully bleached at the other.
- **No step reaches bare bright metal.** Going past full bleaching strips the coating and
  exposes shiny aluminium, which is a third tone that does not belong in the image and reads as
  a blown highlight with a glint. This is the characteristic failure of this work and the first
  thing to look for.
- No banding along the scan direction and no moiré in even tones, at the distance the piece is
  looked at.
- The same wedge reads the same at field centre and near a corner.
- Coating around the image is not crazed, lifted or heat-discoloured.
- Repeatable between runs, and between the first and last piece of a batch.

## How to verify

Burn the ten-step wedge in the same file as the image and judge the wedge first. Tilt the piece
under a single light: bare metal exposed by over-bleaching glints where bleached coating does
not, and that is the only reliable way to see it. Compare centre against a corner.

Coating thickness varies between anodising batches, so the coupon must come from the **same
batch** as the job — more so here than in any other entry, because the whole tonal range is the
coating's thickness.

Dithered and greyscale are separate recipes, as everywhere in this work.
